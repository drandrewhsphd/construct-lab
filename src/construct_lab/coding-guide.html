Curiosity coding guide

Working protocol 0.1 · 8 October 2026 · Based on approved Construct Version 2 · AI pilot completed; human validation pending

1. What this guide does

Use this guide to classify the motivation supported by a particular passage, measure or result. Do not assign a single motive to an entire paper or every participant. This is a working coding protocol derived from approved Construct Version 2, not a validated scale or a new approved definition.

Curiosity is a motivational orientation toward acquiring information or experience that a person perceives as offering new knowledge or understanding.

Boundary: Information seeking alone does not establish curiosity. Reassurance and learning can coexist. Embracing uncertainty is provisional and is not required for a curiosity code.

2. Code two motives before choosing a label

For each unit, mark learning and reassurance/threat management separately as supported, explicitly absent, or not established. Silence is “not established,” not absence. Learning means a supported desire for new knowledge or understanding; merely receiving information or improving performance is insufficient. Reassurance means a supported intention to reduce worry, obtain safety confirmation or manage perceived threat; unpleasant feelings alone are insufficient.

LabelAssign whenWhat it permits you to say
Learning supportedLearning motive is supported; reassurance motive is absent or not established.Evidence is consistent with curiosity under Version 2. Say “learning only” only if reassurance was explicitly ruled out.
Reassurance supportedReassurance/threat-management motive is supported; learning motive is absent or not established.The behavior cannot establish curiosity on this evidence alone. Do not claim curiosity is absent when learning was unmeasured.
Mixed motivesBoth motives are supported for the same person/episode, or are measured as co-occurring within the same defined analytical unit.Curiosity may coexist with reassurance. Record relative strength only when measured; do not invent a dominant motive.
Unclear / otherNeither motive is adequately supported, evidence conflicts, or another motive is supported without either target motive.Retain the behavior as observed; motive remains unclassified. Record other motives, such as external reward or compliance, in a separate note.

3. The decision procedure

  1. Select the unit. Record paper, study, sample/subgroup, task, page and exact supporting passage. Separate theory, measure, result and author interpretation.
  2. Record behavior independently. Was it actual searching, a question asked, a request/intention, willingness to pay, or only a questionnaire response? No observed behavior is also a valid entry.
  3. Record each motive’s evidence. Use the three statuses above and the exact item, report or manipulation supporting each. Record whether motives were measured before or after behavior.
  4. Assign the four-category label. Apply the rules in Section 2. Different subgroups get separate records; a paper containing both groups is not automatically a mixed-motive episode.
  5. Qualify the inference. Distinguish a participant’s reported motive from an author’s proposed explanation or a coder’s interpretation. Preserve null results and exploratory status.
  6. Review uncertainty. If two readings remain plausible, use “unclear,” retain both explanations and flag for adjudication. Do not force agreement or delete the conflicting evidence.

4. Evidence strength and causal limits

Evidence typeHow to use itLimit
Explicit motive report or targeted multi-item measureRecord the supported motive with its wording and timing.Self-report may be biased; a trait measure does not establish the motive of a particular act.
Single-item interest or anxiety ratingRecord what was measured and any author interpretation.Interest in results is not automatically desire to understand; anxiety is not automatically reassurance seeking. A difference-score split is not a pure motive classification.
Experimental manipulationRecord the variable randomized and its effect on the specified outcome.Manipulating threat relevance does not manipulate trait intolerance, embracing or curiosity. Post-treatment exclusions and subgroup analyses require separate notes.
Association or statistical mediationRecord coefficient, uncertainty, covariates and level of analysis.Association and cross-sectional mediation do not establish causal direction. Do not infer individual motives from group correlations.
Task behavior or author label aloneRecord behavior or the author’s terminology verbatim in its own field.Costly seeking, uncertainty reduction, noninstrumental information and the label “curiosity task” do not identify motive by themselves.

Confidence: High = clear, directly relevant motive evidence at the coded level with no material conflict; moderate = relevant but indirect or limited evidence; low = substantial ambiguity or conflicting evidence. Confidence concerns the coding judgment, not the probability that a theory is true. These are working rubric categories, not calibrated scores.

7. Pilot and resolve disagreements

Recommended pilot: Apply the guide to a purposive set of 12 existing excerpts spanning direct learning reports, threat-related seeking, mixed/ambiguous motives and measurement-only findings. Twelve is a practical training batch, not a statistical adequacy threshold. The pilot should include difficult and null-result examples.

If two human coders are available, have them code the same units independently before discussing differences. Keep original codes, report agreement descriptively by category, and adjudicate using source evidence. A small pilot does not establish reliability. An AI-assisted pilot is useful for checking the rules but is not independent human validation. Revise the guide with a new version number when rules change; do not silently recode prior records.

8. What remains open

This guide does not resolve whether curiosity must be intrinsic, whether embracing is a separate facet or enabling condition, or whether results generalize to technology contexts. It also does not establish theoretical saturation. Discomfort from a knowledge gap can accompany curiosity; do not classify all negative affect as reassurance. Retain uncertainty reduction as an observed function when warranted, separately from the person’s motivation.
